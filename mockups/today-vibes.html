Three looks for Orbit’s Today screen

The same Monday drawn three ways. Everything in the phones is clickable: tick things off, tap the weekly goals, stop the timer, poke the moon. All the events and numbers are made up.

Sticker Planner

Your favourite paper planner, but it never runs out of stickers. Ticking something off slaps a puffy star on it and runs a highlighter through it.

Feels like
Dot-grid paper, gel pen, washi tape, an elastic strap. Bedtime turns the page navy.
Try
Tick a task. Tap an empty circle in the sticker chart.
Watch out
Stickers everywhere could turn into scrapbook clutter, so it only stays calm if the page is kept to ink and highlighter.

From a train departure board: times sit in a fixed margin so the day always reads top to bottom.

From a full-screen video feed: only what’s happening now gets the big sticky note.

From an airport gate board: something that changed, like a task carried over, stays highlighted until you’ve dealt with it.

Cosy Quest My pick

Your day as a cosy life-sim. Bean chats to you like a village neighbour, tasks are quests, and finishing them earns stardust.

Feels like
Animal Crossing’s phone, Stardew mornings. Chunky rounded panels on an open sky, and the stars come out at bedtime.
Try
Tick a quest. Tap Bean to hear more. Tap a weekly goal.
Watch out
This is the most familiar of the three: lots of cosy apps look like this. Points and quests could also start to feel like homework.

Island Map

Your week as a bright holiday-park map. Today is the big green island, and each weekly goal is a small island that grows as you make progress.

Feels like
A creased guide map from a summer day out: flat bold colour, no grain, everything bouncy.
Try
Tap a to-do and watch the dot slide across. Tap an island.
Watch out
The boldest and least planner-like of the three. A map shows time less clearly than a list, and the blob shapes take real care to carry over to the calendar and meal screens.

Also considered, and why they lost

  • Split-flap departure board: satisfying clacks, but too cold and stationy to feel cosy.
  • Full-screen swipe feed: one thing at a time, but it’s the doom-scroll shape you’re trying to escape.
  • Annotated old book: beautiful rules and margins, but far too serious.
  • Packed Japanese web page: fits loads on one screen, which is exactly the clutter you don’t want.
  • Boarding pass and gate board: very clear about changes, but feels like a business trip.

Want something classic and clean instead, like Things or Structured? Just say so.
